Looking after yourself

school-refusal

Caring for young people experiencing school attendance challenges can be physically and emotional challenging. They may not want the care you are providing. Or they may not be able to do the things you think will benefit them.

You may feel frustrated, exhausted, lonely or isolated. You may feel that your needs are not as important as your child's needs. You may not even feel entitled to seek or get help. But looking after someone else is not a reason to forget or neglect to look after yourself. If you’re not looking after your own wellbeing, you likely won’t be able to support someone else.

Are you well?

Take a quick 2-minute wellbeing test to check-in on how you’ve been feeling recently. Find out what kind of support you might need right now.

The test is suitable for people aged 18 and over looking to support themselves. Warwick-Edinburgh Mental Wellbeing Scale (see Medicare mental health quiz)

What you can do

Taking care of your mental health and wellbeing can look different to everyone. It's about finding a balance in all areas of your life that suits you. Making small adjustments can help you learn what works for you and stay well.

Where you might take action to stay well:

  • Myself. Am I meeting my physical, mental, social and emotional needs?
  • Employment. Affect work ability..
  • Family. Partner, other children (e.g. strained relationships)

There are many things you can do to look after your mental health and wellbeing. Some are simple, others more challenging. Being aware of them and making a plan is a great place to start.

  • Self-care habits/activities
  • Sleep, keep active, guided meditation, mindfulness etc
  • Take each day as it comes
  • Acknowledge what is and is not in your control
  • Self-care library

You may find it useful to get input from people you trust, or even ask them to join you.

Connect with others

Connecting with other families who understand what you are going through, may help you feel less stressed and alone. And throughout your parenting journey, help you feel more confident and supported.

Not sure where to start, or need help now?

When you’re having a tough time, it’s hard to know what to do or who to turn to for help. You may think nothing will help, or feel embarrassed to ask for help.

Find help immediately

If you are in crisis, anxious, or depressed, and want to talk to someone now, call Lifeline on 13 11 14.

  • ParentLine
  • If you are a carer and you need emergency respite, call Carer Gateway on 1800 422 737.

If it's not an emergency, ReachOut Parents can help you:

  • connect with the Online Community Forum to learn from and share your story with other parents or guardians
  • access free and confidential one-on-one support with a professional parenting and family coach, via phone chat
