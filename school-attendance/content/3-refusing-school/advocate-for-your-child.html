Be your child’s advocate

As an expert of your child, you can be your child’s best advocate.

Being an advocate means promoting and defending another person’s rights, needs and interests.

You may need to advocate for your child when meeting or collaborating with the school.

  • Parent-teacher conferences, student support groups
  • Raising a concern
  • collaborating with the school
  • Making a school attendance plan

See https://raisingchildren.net.au/school-age/school-learning/working-with-schools-teachers/being-an-advocate

How to advocate for your child

- Advocating for a younger child vs older teen
- level of support needed from parent (developmental lens)
- let your child be heard regardless of their ability, develop self-advocacy skills, not making decisions on their behalf
- identify what changes or supports may be helpful, how it helps your child
- responding to resistance: getting information across to help teachers understand, develop the conversation, ask the school "why is this happening?" when there are strategy changes/lapses, give teachers a chance to be heard, keep open communication. Don't be hostile, combative and blacklisted. 
- escalation process

Keep in regular contact with your child’s teacher, or the main contact person at the school, about how your child is doing at school.

Who can support you

You may worry about not being heard or not feel confident to ask for what your child needs. There are people and organisations who can support you, so that you know what to do and say when advocating for your child.

Tell the school if you would like an interpreter or a support person to come to the meeting with you.

Support person

You can have a support person to help you at any time while making a complaint.

The support person can be someone in your family, a friend, community member or from a support agency.

The support person can:

  • help you clarify the issues
  • discuss difficulties you're having about the complaint
  • help create a good working relationship between you and the school
  • help you understand our policies and any resolutions proposed.

Let the principal know you want to use a support person when making the complaint. You should provide their name, contact details and relationship to you.

Interpreter

An interpreter can support communication with the school and help you to relax and express yourself comfortably.

If you need an interpreter to talk to your school, call the National Translating and Interpreting Service on 131 450. For further information, please refer to the department's Interpreting and Translation Services page.

If you are unhappy with the school’s support

If you aren’t satisfied with the way a staff member is responding to your concerns, ask for assistance from another staff member, or escalate to a more senior staff member.

After talking to the school, you can complain if you are unhappy with the support you or your child are getting at school, or if your concern is about the principal and you don't want to talk to them directly.

As a parent or carer, you can also authorise someone to raise a complaint on your behalf, such as a support person or advocate.

To make a complaint about:

  • Victorian government schools, contact the Department of Education
  • Catholic schools, contact the Victorian Catholic Education Authority
  • independent schools, contact Victoria Registration and Qualifications Authority (VRQA).

Victorian government schools and school staff

Speak to the department’s regional office

Contact your closest regional office for questions, feedback or assistance in working with the school.

You can call the department's regional offices on 1800 338 663 or email enquiries@education.vic.gov.au.

Ask for a Central Office Review

If you have received a response from the regional office, and are still unhappy with the outcome, you can contact the central office.consider whether any of the decisions or actions taken by the school or regional office were unreasonable or incorrect in relation to department policy. The central office can also refer eligible complaints to the Independent Office for School Dispute Resolution.

You can also contact the central office by:

  • completing an online form
  • emailing school.complaints@education.vic.gov.au
  • phoning (03) 8688 7885 (message call-back service)
  • mailing the below postal address

Deputy Secretary, Schools and Regional Services
c/o Manager, Complaints and Improvement Unit
GPO Box 4367 Melbourne VIC 3001

If you are not satisfied with the department's response

If you are unhappy with the department's response, you can contact the Victorian Ombudsman by phone (03) 9613 6222 or via their website.
