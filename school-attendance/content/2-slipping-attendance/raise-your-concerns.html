Raise your concerns

The school should be the first point of contact if your child has difficulty attending school. Often, talking with your child's teacher is the best way to start. Tell them what is happening and ask for their support. Contacting the school early will also reduce the chances of the problem becoming more severe.

Ask to speak to someone in the school. This may be your child’s:

  • class teacher
  • year level coordinator
  • learning and wellbeing coordinator
  • guidance counsellor
  • a trusted teacher
  • principal or vice-principal.
My local school contacts form

This forms allows you to create a list of local school contacts. It’ll also help to find out who’s responsible for what in your child’s school, the school’s governing body, or other services your child uses. This way you’ll know who to talk to and what you can expect.

Let the school know your child

It takes time for teachers and other school staff to become familiar with all students. You can help the school get to know your child by sharing their preferences, strengths and abilities. This information lets teachers plan for ways to best engage and support your child's success in school.

Get to know me form

Create a learner's profile and share it with the school. Use it to identify discussion points before a meeting or conversation, and seek support from school staff.

Tell them your concerns

It's always okay to talk to the school about your child's wellbeing or learning.

However, it is best to make an appointment time with school staff and tell them what you would like to discuss, so that you can meet and have sufficient time to talk about your concern.

  • Be clear about the issues you want to discuss. It may be helpful to write down what you want to say beforehand and bring your notes, such as a completed attendance diary or get to know me form with you to the meeting.
  • Tell the school what you know. Let them know about your child’s feelings and the specific things that they are finding difficult.
  • Ask what is happening at school. For example, has the teacher noticed anything that your child finds particularly challenging? Are there sudden changes in their school performance or behaviours?
  • Be open and honest. Share with the school any information that may provide clues about the reasons for your child’s school attendance. For example, changes at home or about family members.
  • Work with school staff. Look for patterns in your child’s absences from school (e.g. certain days of the week, after lunch).
Asking for the school’s support script

This script is for raising a concern, sharing information or seeking support from your child's school.

For urgent matters or if someone requires immediate support, always call the school and speak to school staff. Otherwise, allow time for a staff member to get back to you.

Managing the school's response

Sometimes you may need to meet with the school again to resolve the matter. They may need more time to observe your child, talk to others or examine past records, to figure out what’s going on.

At the end of the initial meeting, if the matter is unresolved, lock in a second appointment? Ask if they will remain as the key school contact person so that you don’t have to repeat yourself? You may find it helpful to ask for assistance from another staff member

If you aren’t satisfied with the way a staff member is responding to your concerns, ask for assistance from another staff member, or escalate to a more senior staff member.

Asking to meet with the school and for their support can be daunting. Or you might face the school’s resistance. You may find it useful to get support from people you trust, so that you can be your child’s best advocate.

After talking to the school, you may have figured out the barriers to your child's attendance. Together with your child, identify things that may help them at school.

Case studies

Case studies of reasons, feelings, thoughts and possible solutions?

Reason

Child has difficulty following spoken instructions and misses a piece of information given. Teacher yells at them for 'not paying attention' and being the only student to read the wrong text.

Feeling
I am confused about what to do in class because the teacher speaks too fast. I feel embarrassed for being yelled at and singled out in class. Thoughts I hate this teacher.

Possible solutions

  • Provide written and spoken instructions.
  • Simplify instructions. Speak clearly and give limited information each time. Check for understanding before moving on to the next step.
  • Check in with student to ensure understanding
  • Ask teacher to be kind, approachable and easy to talk to.
